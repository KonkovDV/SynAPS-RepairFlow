# SOTA 2026 positioning

## Problem class

RepairFlow is a constrained multi-resource maintenance scheduler with precedence, alternative posts, qualified crews, tooling, setup times, calendars, spares, and disruption replanning. The current implementation intentionally supports linear technology cards; convergent DAG repair flows remain outside the supported contract until a compiler is implemented.

## Evidence-backed positioning

- Constraint programming is a credible industrial approach for operational maintenance scheduling with precedence, deadlines, resources, and lexicographic objectives. See Sciau et al., 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jairtraman.2024.102537).
- Independent validation is the correct trust boundary for comparing heuristics and exact solvers. A 2026 technician-constrained maintenance benchmark explicitly uses a validator separate from every scheduling method. See [SSRN 7095162](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7095162).
- MUS/MCS-style explanations are a credible next step for disrupted industrial schedules. See Bleukx et al., CP 2025, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CP.2025.6).
- Learned dispatching and uncertainty-aware methods are research directions, not reasons to claim AI capability. They require data-calibrated evaluation, transfer tests, and a hard-constraint validator.

## What this project should claim

The defensible contribution is systems engineering: an open domain contract, deterministic compilation into a pinned engine, independent fail-closed validation, reproducible evidence, and a human-controlled shadow workflow. It is not a claim of a new optimization algorithm or state-of-the-art solver performance.

## Benchmark plan

For a future paper-grade evaluation, publish: instance generator and all seeds; exact environment manifest; independent validator; FIFO/EDD/list-scheduling baselines; CP-SAT reference; rolling-horizon disruption scenarios; feasibility, coverage, tardiness, makespan, setup, stability, runtime, and manual-edit metrics; and confidence intervals across repeated seeds.
